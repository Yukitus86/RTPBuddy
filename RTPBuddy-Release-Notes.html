The thread running through it: the sitting

Four of the fourteen releases circle the same thing. A sitting is one stretch of play, and you want to be able to name it, continue it, fold several together, and trust the numbers on it. Up to 1.5.8 a sitting was simply whatever happened between logging in and logging out.

On numbering: every landing carries two. The global one lives in the file, stays unique and is never reused — the CSV, the viewer and the delete dialog all mean that one. What is shown instead is the position within the sitting, as soon as only one sitting is on the plot. Since 1.6.2 the plot decides that, not the view you happen to have picked.

1.6.16

8 Sep 2026

A tile is a piece of ground. It should not grow just because you zoomed out.

Changed

The counted tiles stay small out to 100k

Tile size followed the zoom, staying about the same width on screen however far out you went. That looked identical at every zoom — which was exactly the problem, because the ground behind one square quadrupled along the way. At a 50k scale bar a square stood for 32,000 blocks a side, at 100k for 64,000. The question a tile asks — has anything ever landed in here — is worth nothing at that size.

The size is held now: 8,000 blocks a side until the scale bar passes 100k. Zooming out shrinks the squares instead of coarsening them. At 50k they are 14 pixels rather than 58 and still carry their count; only past that are they colour bands alone. From 100k the old growing tile takes back over — the held one falls under three pixels there and could not be drawn at all.

Set it under Map → Tile size: 2,000 · 4,000 · 8,000 · 16,000 · 32,000 blocks. The automatic size is still the ceiling: close in, an 8k tile would fill a third of the screen and the picture would stop being a raster.

Fixed

The raster went blank at a wide zoom

The raster draws at most 20,000 tiles and gives up entirely past that. Small tiles on a wide view cross it easily — at a 100k scale bar it would have been 22,000 — and nearly all of them were ground far outside the recording that the loop would have skipped anyway. It now runs only over what the mask covers: 22,000 becomes 3,600, and the picture stays up.

1.6.15

8 Sep 2026

A setting you have to make again on every open is not a setting.

Fixed

The map opened on Panels again, whatever had been picked

Tab and the panel button cycle four states: Panels, Stats, List and Map — the last one clears both sidebars and gives the plot the whole window. The state lived only in the map screen, and that screen is rebuilt constantly: every toolbar press rebuilds it, and a landing throws it away and puts a new one in its place. So every close and reopen was back to Panels.

The choice now sits in config.json under map.panelMode, written the moment it is changed — not on close, because a teleport tears the map down without ever going through there.

A window too narrow for the chosen panels still shows fewer. That applies to the one layout pass only and is never written back: otherwise a single resize would destroy the setting for good.

Changed

The search box survives a close

Dimension, region, capture mode, biome and the ticked sittings were all still set on the next open — the search text was the one thing that was not. It now rides along with Remember filter and is stored in map.filterSearch.

1.6.14

6 Sep 2026

Nobody sends a run out to find “something rare”.

New

The search order finds single biomes

The eighteen biome families were tick rows: switch on Rare and the run stopped on mushroom fields, a cherry grove or a pale garden. A family is a colour band on the map, and for that it is right. For a search order it is the wrong grain — what is being hunted is the Pale Garden, and the family is only how you get there.

Every family row now opens its biomes: the whole family on top as before, and under it every single biome with the number of landings already recorded in it. Several may be ticked at once, in one family or across many, and the menu stays open while you tick them — three biomes are three ticks, not three trips through the same menu. The row itself then says what is picked: off, whole family, the biome's name, or a count.

The list comes from the server's own biome registry, not from your landings. That is the whole point: a list built from the recordings holds exactly the biomes you already have, and therefore never the one you are looking for. Recorded biomes are folded in on top, so a name cannot vanish because you happen to be on a different server. Which makes the 0 behind a biome the interesting number: that is where the search has never been.

The value already lived in config.json as autoRtp.findBiomes and was already tested against — all that was missing was any way to set it with a mouse.

Fixed

The mangrove swamp was a snow biome

Families are decided by substrings, and the rule for grove matched in the middle of mangrove_swamp. The mangrove swamp had been sitting in the snow since 1.6.10: pale blue dot on the map, wrong row in the biome breakdown, wrong family in the search order.

It turned up while counting for the new menu — all 65 biomes of this version pushed through the classifier once and looked at. The rule now sits above the grove rule, which is where the file already asks narrower rules to go.

1.6.13

6 Sep 2026

Eighteen choices do not belong on a cycle button.

Changed

The biome filter drops down a list

The toolbar's other filters cycle, which is right for three or four choices: one press moves a step, the next takes it back. With eighteen biome families it stops being a filter — after the wrong pick there are seventeen more clicks between you and all biomes again.

The button now opens a list under itself: All biomes on top, below it only the families the chosen scope actually holds, each with its colour swatch and its count. One click picks, a click beside it closes, and so does Esc. The current choice is highlighted.

The list is built from the whole scope, not from the filtered set: once a family is picked the filtered set holds exactly that one, and a list built from it could never offer another — the same bug the dimension filter had before 1.6.2.

1.6.12

6 Sep 2026

A full map becomes a picture rather than twenty thousand rectangles.

Changed

The markers and the route go into a texture

Everything the map draws goes through a single rectangle call, and on 1.21.11 each of those allocates twice: a render state and a copy of the current matrix. A round marker is five of them, a hollow one eight, a route leg one more plus a matrix push and rotate. Two thousand framed landings came to some twenty thousand rectangles a frame — which is not arithmetic the machine minds, it is allocation it does.

Over the drawing budget the whole layer — every landing and the route between them — is painted into a texture instead, and every frame in between is a single quad. It is repainted only when something it depends on moves: the view, the filter, the marker mode, the colour mode, the picked landing. Not the mouse. Measured on 1971 landings: 0.44 ms without the route, 0.86 ms with it and its chevrons.

The happy side effect is that inside a texture a marker costs pixels rather than draw calls, which makes the thinning from 1.6.11 unnecessary: the dense case draws every landing round again, with the whole route. It looks better than it did and is still faster.

Two fallbacks. On a very large canvas — over 2.2 million pixels — handing the picture to the GPU costs more than the rectangles it saves, so the map goes back to drawing and thinning them. And a repaint that turns out to take longer than 2.5 ms may be outrun by a view still being dragged: the previous picture stands for up to 80 ms rather than the drag stuttering. The same rule the gap field already used.

What stays outside is what the picture cannot hold: the picked and hovered landings, which change with a click and with the mouse, and the sample numbers, which need the font.

Changed

The metrics column only draws what is on screen

The column is one long strip cut off with a scissor, and every row was drawn whether or not it was in the window. With the cell board and the two biome breakdowns that runs to a good hundred and twenty rows, each a handful of rectangles and two pieces of text, so the rows scrolled past cost as much as the ones being read.

Now only what is in the window is drawn. The cursor still walks all of them: the skipped rows have to keep their height, or the scrollbar would not know how far the column runs.

1.6.11

6 Sep 2026

First pass at what a full map costs, measured rather than guessed.

Fixed

The route allocated three arrays per segment

Clipping a line to the canvas built three double[4] every time: the two Liang-Barsky tables and the result. At two thousand segments that is six thousand arrays a frame to hand back four numbers, plus one more array per chevron.

The galling part: it ran even when no line was visible. Zoomed right in, the route cost close to six thousand allocations a frame for nothing. The result now lives in fields and the clip allocates none.

New

Drawing budget

One number under Map → Performance: how many markers may be on screen at once before the map draws the cheap way. 500 by default; Off draws every landing on its own again.

What is counted is what is actually on screen, not how large the file is: two thousand landings zoomed in on one of them is not a dense plot and should cost nothing.

Changed

No chevrons on a full route

Each chevron is two more rotated rectangles, two thirds of what the line itself costs. On two thousand overlapping segments nobody reads them as direction anyway, only as noise. Past 400 segments they are dropped; the line still fades from old to new, which is the same information at none of the price.

1.6.10

6 Sep 2026

Three things that make the recording more than a scatter of dots: a target, a search order, and the biome.

New

Cell board: 22 of 81

The metrics column now opens with a nine-by-nine board, one square per server cell. A cell holding landings is filled in its zone's colour, more of it the more it holds; a cell with exactly one carries a gold ring, because a single deleted landing would empty it again. The cell you are standing in is ringed in green. Under it sit the per-zone bars — 7/10, 6/27 — each with the numbers of the cells that zone is still missing.

This is the coverage figure the percentage beside it cannot be. That one divides by a disc whose size depends on the cell edge you happen to have set, so it only says something about itself. The grid has 81 cells whatever anyone configures — 22 of 81 is a sentence with a finish line in it.

A zone with no landings at all is drawn dim and sorted to the bottom. That is not a gap in the map but a zone never asked for, and listing its cells as missing would put fifty squares on a to-do list nothing has ever tried to reach.

/rtpbuddy cells prints the same to chat. A landing in a cell never reached before says so in chat and pulses that cell on the minimap for a few seconds.

New

Search order: the run stops when it finds something

Auto-RTP used to stop for damage, the run timer, the session cap and a guard — every one a reason to give up. A search order is the other kind: a reason to stop because the run found what it was sent out for. It keeps teleporting until a landing matches, stops on that landing, plays a short bell and says in chat which condition fired.

On offer: a cell never reached, a typed list of named cells, eighteen biome families, a distance band from the origin, and a target point with a radius. Combine decides whether one satisfied condition is enough or all have to hold at once, and Give up after ends a run that has turned down that many landings — without it an order for a rare target runs until something else stops it.

None of this makes the loop send more, faster or differently. A search order can only end a run earlier than it would have ended anyway. While it is searching the HUD says what for, and how many landings have been turned down.

New

The biome becomes visible

Every landing has carried its biome since schema 3, and for a long time nobody could see it anywhere. Now in three places: a colour mode Biome, a filter button in the toolbar, and two breakdowns in the metrics column — the families first, then the eight busiest single biomes.

Colouring is by biome family, not by biome: close to fifty distinct ones are recorded, and fifty hues on one plot is not a key but confetti. Names come from Minecraft's own translations, so not one biome name ships in this mod's language files, and the search box matches both the id and the shown name — Kirschhain finds what is stored as minecraft:cherry_grove.

New

A new cell announces itself

Land in a cell no recorded landing has ever reached and the chat says so with the new standing — 22 of 81 — while the cell pulses on the minimap for eight seconds. Both hang off the landing itself rather than off a per-frame question: the map would otherwise have walked the whole dataset every frame to answer something that comes up once per landing.

New

/rtpbuddy cells and /rtpbuddy find

cells prints the cell progress to chat, zone by zone with the missing numbers, and ends with the cell you are standing in. find shows the search order; find on and find off switch it.

1.6.9

6 September 2026

The map is now available small, in a corner, without anything having to stop for it.

New

A minimap in the corner

The full map answers where you are, and charges a key press and a paused view for it. The minimap answers the same question without either: a small plate at the edge of the screen framing exactly the rectangle F frames on the full map, with your own marker inside it.

It is not a terrain minimap and does not try to be one. What it draws is the recording: the 50,000 block server cells in the colour of the region that owns them, their numbers written in where they fit, the landings as single pixels, the red leg into the newest one, and the world axes. Underneath sits a strip naming the cell you are standing in — region and server number, in that region's colour — and how much ground the plate covers.

The frame is fixed. It holds the chosen landings and does not scroll with you, because a map that recentres on every step is precisely the one that says nothing about where you are in the recording. Walk out of the rectangle and your marker is pinned to the rim in amber rather than drawn where nobody can see it. With nothing recorded it frames the server cell you are standing in, which is still an answer.

And it costs next to no frame rate. The plate is painted once into a texture and then drawn as a single quad. A thousand landings would otherwise be a thousand fill calls in every single frame — the mistake the gap map very nearly shipped with, made permanent, because a minimap is on screen for the whole session. It is repainted only on a new landing, a different sitting, a switch flipped, or crossing into another cell; that measures under 3 ms with two thousand landings on it. Per frame what is left is the marker, the cell numbers and the caption, some fifty rectangles.

Bottom right by default, because the text overlay sits in the top left and another mod's minimap usually claims the top right. Moved like the overlay, and switched on and off from /rtpbuddy minimap as well.

Changed

The placement screen places both

It used to hold one box. Now it holds both: the text overlay and the minimap, each labelled, with Tab switching between them. That is not convenience — the question this screen exists to answer is does anything overlap here, and you cannot answer it with only one of two boxes on the screen.

Both are always shown, switched off or not: placing something before turning it on is the ordinary order, and a box that vanished when its switch was off would be unplaceable exactly when it needed placing.

1.6.8

4 September 2026

Three map modes instead of five. The new one does what the two old ones did, and answers the question better.

Removed

Heatmap and cluster

Both said the same thing: where is it dense. The heatmap painted density as colour, cluster folded markers into bubbles — two answers to one question, and both about the dots rather than the ground. That was exactly the problem: a dark patch on a heatmap meant "never landed there", "hidden by the filter" and "the region does not reach that far", all at once.

The gap mode measures the distance to the nearest landing instead. The statement no longer lives in an absence of colour but in a number that can be shaded, ranked and read off — and once that existed, the other two had nothing left to say.

The mode button now goes Points → Path → Advanced. A config still holding HEATMAP or CLUSTER quietly falls back to points on load; nothing breaks and nothing is lost.

The Heatmap cell setting is now the Density cell and draws nothing. It stays because it is the basis of the coverage statistic — cells hit divided by the cells inside the border.

Changed

The gap mode is called Advanced

It was Dummy. The name was aimed at people who could not make sense of the other modes — it is now the mode that does the most, and a name claiming the opposite gets in the way of that.

Internally the mode stays GAPS, so a stored map.markerMode never has to be touched.

1.6.7

4 September 2026

Auto-RTP without the clock: the same run, but you are the trigger.

New

Trigger by hand

A switch in the Auto-RTP tab turns the clock off. The run stays a run — the rotation, the counters, the caps, the run timer and every stop condition behave exactly as before — but nothing is sent until you press the auto-RTP key, one teleport per press.

It is for the other half of the job: filling a map and looking at where you landed want different rhythms, and the second one is yours to set rather than a rate you had to guess in advance. Cooldown and jitter no longer apply, bar a hard floor of 2 seconds between commands — a held key must not turn into a command every tick.

A press that cannot be honoured refuses rather than queues, and says why. A teleport fired silently later would be worse than none at all: knowing when the command went out is the entire point of stepping by hand. A press that worked says nothing — the command is already in the chat, the server answers it, and the HUD counts it.

There is no countdown, so none is shown: the HUD reads MANUAL and the map button Auto manual. Plus /rtpbuddy auto step.

1.6.6

4 September 2026

The gap map from 1.6.5 sharpened by real use, and the key bindings cut back to what is actually needed.

New

Red line into the last landing

Every plot now draws one line without being asked: from the point the newest teleport started at to the point it landed on — in red, with an arrow the way it went. Watching the map, it answers which of the dots you just came from without a click.

Drawn from that sample's recorded origin rather than from the sample before it: in between you walk, you fall, you take a portal, and only the recorded origin is a fact about that teleport. Left out across dimensions, where a straight line measures nothing. Switched off as map.showLastLeg.

Changed

Seven keys down to five, one carries the whole run

The auto-RTP key now does all of it: no run → start dialog, a run on the clock → hold and continue, a run stepped by hand → send the next teleport. Stopping is End's job alone — the one irreversible action sits on a key of its own rather than one press away from a hold.

Gone are the separate key for the hold and the one for the capture pause: a switch you set once a session does not need a key, and a second key that only half-stopped the loop was one too many to keep straight in a hurry. Both still work from /rtpbuddy capture pause, /rtpbuddy auto pause and the settings.

Changed

The gap mask hugs the recording

1.6.5 grew every occupied tile by a ring — which wrapped the whole recording in an apron of never-visited ground several tiles wide. Honestly empty and completely useless: empty because nobody has looked there yet, not because the server avoids it.

The mask is now the recording's own footprint, filled rather than grown. The tile size is only a strip width: a point is inside when it lies between the leftmost and rightmost landing of its row strip and between the topmost and bottommost of its column strip — measured against real coordinates, not tile edges. Against 1,201 landings: from 76 % of the plot down to 17 %.

A hole at the frontier is still listed. The field goes on rising into unexplored ground, so no cell inside the mask is a local maximum there; a neighbour outside no longer counts as higher. Without that, the biggest hole on the map was the one that went missing from the list. Default strip width 8,000 → 16,000, choices 4k to 64k.

Fixed

Outer tiles blinked while the map was dragged

The gap mode asked, per tile, "is the centre inside the mask?" - and took the centre clipped to the screen. On a tile hanging half off the edge that point walks across the tile as you drag, while the tile stands still. The same square answered differently from one frame to the next.

The mask now survives the build as a world-space statement and is asked at the tile's real middle. On top of that, a tile holding a landing is drawn whatever the mask says: the mask is not tile-aligned, so a tile whose middle fell just outside it could still hold landings in a corner — markers on bare ground with no tile under them, which looked like a bug and was one.

1.6.5

4 September 2026

A fifth map mode that asks the opposite question: not where the landings are, but where they are not.

New

Map mode Dummy — the gap map

Points, path, heatmap and cluster all draw the same subject: the landings. That leaves a gap as nothing more than ground with nothing drawn on it — and a dark patch on a heatmap means three things at once: never landed there, hidden by the filter, or the region does not reach that far. Nothing on screen tells them apart.

The new mode shades every point of the plot by the distance to the nearest recorded landing: dark where they are dense, amber where nothing has ever come up. The absence itself becomes a value that can be shaded, ranked and read off. The biggest holes are ringed, numbered and listed with their coordinates and radius in the metrics column, and the cursor line reads out the distance under the pointer.

Two pictures, one measurement: up to the 5k scale the field is shaded, because close in a hole is a shape with edges. Past it the same ground is cut into fixed world tiles carrying their own landing count in four bands — none, one, two to three, four and more — because a shape a few pixels across is not a shape and a number is. It is the same 5k landmark the sample numbers took in 1.6.4, so the map has one rule rather than two. Tiles snap to fixed world coordinates, so the same tile means the same square between sittings and can be ticked off a list.

Distance on its own would always name the edge of the canvas as the emptiest place, since nothing has ever been recorded past it. The measurement is bounded by a mask: the world tiles holding a landing, grown by one tile outward. Inside it, “there is nothing here” says something about the server; outside it, only something about how far the recording has got — and that is dimmed so you can see which is which.

Filter to one region first. The measurement handles overlap correctly on its own — a spot two regions reach is only rare if it is rare in both. But a hole in the region you care about will be filled in by landings of a region you never asked for there.

A reported radius is always rounded down: the patch is at least that empty. The field is measured on a screen-space grid, and a cell is worth a few hundred blocks with a whole region in view — the figure would rather understate by that much than send you to a clearing with a landing sitting in the middle of it.

New

Two settings for it

Gap search area (2,000 to 32,000 blocks, default 8,000) sets the tile the map uses to mark off where anything has been recorded at all — bigger looks further past the outermost landings. Listed gaps (3, 5, 8 or 12) says how many holes get ringed and listed.

One note on the plumbing, because it is what makes the mode usable at all: the shading is uploaded as a texture once per rebuild and then drawn as one quad. The obvious way — a rectangle per cell — is the wrong one on 1.21.11: every fill allocates a render-state object and a copy of the matrix, so three thousand cells is six thousand allocations on every frame of a zoom. That is exactly what heated up a CPU before the mode shipped.

1.6.4

4 September 2026

Sharpening the plot numbers from 1.6.3.

Changed

Numbers only from the 5k scale onward

Crowding alone was not enough of a threshold: with widely spread landings the numbers still turned up too early. The scale bar in the bottom left now decides first — while it reads more than 5,000 blocks the numbers stay off.

Zoomed in past that, the crowding check from 1.6.3 applies as well. The rule now sits visibly on the screen instead of in a zoom figure nobody can read off.

1.6.3

4 September 2026

Sittings can now be folded together and given names. Plus two small things that got in the way daily: the numbers on the plot, and the fixed rate steps.

New

Merge sessions

Tick two or more sittings in the session list, then Merge sessions (n). The target is the oldest: its colour, its name and its start time stay, and its span widens to cover them all.

Nothing is deleted. Every landing survives and belongs to one sitting afterwards, numbered from 1 in the order you actually landed. Checked against a real file of 900 landings: 344 + 190 came out as 1…534, no gaps, in timestamp order.

The other session records go, so a dialog asks first — it cannot be undone. Refused across servers. If the running sitting was among them, it carries on inside the merged one.

New

Rename session

Tick exactly one sitting, then Rename session — the running one included. Up to 32 characters, instead of every row repeating donutsmp.net.

The name then shows in the session list, in the session card and in the viewer. Leave it empty to clear it and get the server name back.

Changed

Sample numbers only once zoomed in

Zoomed far out the numbers piled on top of each other and hid the very markers they were labelling. They now stay off until there is room for them.

What is measured is not the zoom level but how tightly the markers sit: if more than a fifth of them would share a label-sized patch, the numbers stay off. For the fixed floor added on top of it, see 1.6.4.

Changed

Rate typed freely, floor 2 s

Cooldown and jitter in the Auto-RTP tab are input fields rather than fixed steps — no ladder of values hits the interval a given server actually wants.

The floor is 2 s instead of 5. It still never shortens a server cooldown: if the server refuses, the next attempt simply waits the same interval again.

1.6.2

4 September 2026

Follow-up to 1.6.1: the numbering now follows what is actually on the plot.

Fixed

A single ticked sitting started mid-count

Ticking exactly one sitting in All sessions shows one sitting — but the numbers began at the global figure, so somewhere in the middle.

What decides now is the plot, not the view you picked: with one sitting on it, it counts from 1. Only with two does the global number take over, because only that one stays unique then.

Changed

Search matches both numbers

You see #3 and type 3 — and the file holds that landing as #297. The search now matches both, so neither way of remembering a sample comes up empty.

1.6.1

4 September 2026

The session list remembers what is ticked, and an earlier sitting can be continued instead of a new one being started.

Fixed

Ticked sittings were reset when the map closed

The picking is now saved (map.filterSessions), and on every click rather than only on close: a teleport can tear the map down without it ever closing properly.

A picking whose sittings no longer exist is dropped when the map opens — otherwise you would face an empty plot with no visible cause.

Fixed

A picking also emptied the “This session” view

If the picking did not contain the running sitting, the single sitting plot came out empty too. The session filter now bites only in All sessions, and is parked while the map shows one sitting.

New

Continue session

Tick exactly one earlier sitting, then Continue session: every further landing is added to it, the count carries on rather than restarting at 1, and the HUD counts on from that sitting's total.

The sitting in progress is closed first — and discarded if it recorded nothing. Refused across servers. A relog deliberately ends it: logging back in opens a new sitting, and continuing again is one click.

1.6.0

4 September 2026

Auto-RTP can be held rather than only stopped, and the start dialog no longer demands a tick box.

New

N holds auto-RTP without ending the run

For the moment you want to look at wherever the last teleport landed. The cooldown and the run timer both freeze, the counters and the position in the region rotation stay put, and damage or guards stop nothing while it holds — what you take standing around is yours, not the loop's business.

N again picks up with the wait that was left. Also via /rtpbuddy auto pause and resume, or the button in the Auto-RTP tab. The HUD reads HELD in grey and the map button Auto held — a countdown that never moves would look like a stuck game. H and End still end it outright.

Changed

Start dialog without the acknowledgement tick

The “I checked: repeating is allowed here” box is gone. The dialog still names the whole rotation, the cooldown with its jitter and every condition that ends the run — nothing is sent before you press Start Auto-RTP. Pressing it is the confirmation.

The button stays greyed out only when no chosen region has a command, because the loop would then send nothing.

Removed

The N key for “map, all sessions”

The map remembers its own scope now that the scope is saved with the rest — so both keys opened the same screen. The freed key went to the hold above.

All sessions is still reachable from the button in the top right of the map and via /rtpbuddy all.

1.5.9

3 September 2026

Finding yourself on the plot, and being able to begin a new sitting without logging out and back in.

New

Player marker redrawn

With the map open the green dot was barely visible during a teleport. Now: a ring with a dark collar that separates from whatever it lands on, a slow outward ripple (map.playerPulse) and a one-pixel stub from its centre for the direction you face.

Motion is the one channel nothing else on the plot uses — after a landing you find yourself again at once.

New

Start a new sitting

A button at the foot of the session card closes the sitting in progress and opens the next one, without relogging. Samples then count from 1 again, and the card's counters reset with them.

Nothing is deleted: the landings recorded so far keep their sitting. A sitting with no landing is left alone — otherwise you would swap an empty one for an identical empty one.

Fixed

Viewer showed the region as a raw id

The viewer's hover card printed na_east rather than NA East. It goes through the same labelling rule as the mod now.
